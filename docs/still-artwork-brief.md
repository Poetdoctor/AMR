# Still-narrative artwork — illustration brief

Eleven square illustrations, one per beat of the Home narrative.

This is a brief for an illustrator. It assumes no knowledge of the
project or the codebase.

**A set drawn to this brief already ships.** See
[`still-artwork/`](still-artwork/) for the eleven images and how they
were made. This brief remains the specification: anything replacing
them is held to it, and the notes below on individual beats are the
part most easily lost in a redraw.

## What this is for

The site's home page tells a story in eleven beats, drawn from
interviews with two patients living with antimicrobial-resistant
infections and with the clinicians who treat them. It is a Human
Practices project by iGEM UBC: a student team asking what resistant
infection does to a person, rather than what it does to a bacterial
population.

The story exists in two forms. Readers on a capable device travel
through a 3D scene as they scroll. Everyone else — anyone on a low-end
phone, anyone whose device asks for reduced motion, anyone with
JavaScript off, and every search engine — gets the **still version**:
the same eleven beats, the same words, with one illustration beside
each.

**The still version is not a downgrade and must not look like one.** It
is a first-class path in this project, and it is what the readers with
the least to spend will see. These eleven images are the entire visual
argument for those people.

Each illustration sits in a square card beside the beat's heading, its
fragment words, a quote from a real person, and a paragraph of prose.
It supports that text. It is not asked to carry the meaning alone.

## Art direction

**Abstract, not literal.** No hospital rooms, no equipment rendered
faithfully, no scenes you could identify a ward from. The existing
drawings are geometry and light — a tilted path, a rising line, panes
of glass, a field of distant points. Keep that register. A beat about
isolation does not need to depict a hospital to land.

**One recurring figure.** A simple, featureless human form appears in
most beats: a circle and a rounded rectangle, no face, no gender, no
age, no skin tone. It should be anybody. Where it appears, it is the
same form each time, and its posture carries the beat — leaning,
sinking, walled in, and finally surrounded rather than alone. Do not
give it features in a later beat because the composition wants them.

**Restraint over drama.** This is a project about people not being
heard. Nothing here should be frightening, pitying, or medical-horror.
The quotes are already the strongest thing on the page. Illustration
that competes with them is illustration that has misread the room.

**Consistent as a set.** These are seen in sequence as somebody
scrolls. They should read as eleven views of one world, not eleven
separate commissions. Shared palette, shared line weight, shared
treatment of the figure, shared sense of scale.

### Palette

Use these values. They are the site's, and the card sits directly on
the second one.

| Role | Hex |
| --- | --- |
| Page ground | `#faf5ea` |
| Card ground (what these sit on) | `#f4ecdb` |
| Warm neutral surface | `#f0e7d6` |
| Hairlines, edges | `#e2d5bd` |
| Darkest ink — use sparingly | `#231b12` |
| Primary drawing tone | `#4e4135` |
| Secondary / receding tone | `#6a5941` |
| Accent — the single warm note | `#b04a22` |
| Deep accent | `#8f3a16` |
| Light accent | `#e58a55` |

The accent is an accent. In the current drawings the figure carries it
and almost nothing else does. One warm element per image is usually
right; three is usually wrong.

No colour outside this list. In particular, no clinical blue-green —
the site deliberately has none, and one image introducing it will read
as stock.

### Type

None. No words, numbers, or lettering anywhere in the artwork. The
beat's words are set in live text next to it, in five languages, and a
word baked into an image is a word that stays English for four of them.

## The eleven beats

Each entry gives the beat's heading, the fragment words set beside it,
and the sentence the beat is built on. Where a quote is shown, those
are a real person's words, exactly as they said them.

The "currently" line describes the existing drawing. It is there as a
starting point, not a constraint — the whole point of this commission
is that these can be better.

---

**1. The fall** · *Hope · Again? · Fear*

> "A sinking feeling is what you feel first: I've had this before and
> oh no, it's happening again." — Norma Washburn

Nothing dramatic happens. There is no moment anybody would put in a
chart. There is a result, or a phrase, or a look — and the ground is
not quite where it was a second ago.

*Currently:* a narrow path tapering to a vanishing point, the whole
frame tilted three degrees, the figure leaning with it.

---

**2. The rollercoaster** · *Happiness · Despair · Encouraged · Back in
the dumps*

Initial hope, then despair, then encouragement, and ultimately back
down. Patients described the same shape again and again. It is not a
description of an illness — it is what living with one that keeps
returning does to a person.

*Currently:* a wave beneath the figure; the ground moves and the person
does not.

---

**3. The invisible weight** · *Another course · And another · Enough*

Every course of antibiotics is one more thing to get through. They
accumulate. Nobody counts them, because each one on its own is
reasonable.

*Currently:* repeated marks stacking up, and the figure bending under
the accumulation rather than under any one of them.

---

**4. The hospital that doesn't see you** · *Diagnosis · Prescription ·
Test · Procedure*

> "I want others to understand how I feel and stop long enough to allow
> me to share how I feel instead of going straight into…the treatment"

Everyone here is competent, and everyone here is busy. Canada has a
physician shortage, British Columbia included, and finding time to
connect personally is genuinely hard. **This beat is not about
villains.** Nobody in it is doing anything wrong.

*Currently:* many figures in motion, one still, nobody stopping.

---

**5. The missing explanation** · *Why this? · Why now? · Nobody said*

> "They are busy putting in a PICC telling me that I need it, but only
> because I have some nursing experience I understood it… I would be
> really really scared otherwise… people have no time to explain to
> patients."

Swabs, lines, scans, results. Each has a reason, and the reason stays
inside the machine.

*Currently:* something large overhead, arriving on its own schedule,
the person beneath it small.

---

**6. Isolation** · *Am I dangerous? · Am I contagious? · Am I dirty?*

The room does not change. Staff, then family, then friends start
putting on gowns and gloves before coming in, and then it is a
different room.

The people outside are still there, still close enough to see. They did
not leave. That is the thing this beat needs: separation, not
abandonment.

*Currently:* panes forming one after another between the figure and
everyone else.

---

**7. The loneliness** · *Nobody else · Anywhere · Surely somebody*

Both patients told us, separately, that no community of people with
resistant infections exists to connect with, and both said they would
value one.

*Currently:* dark water, one small island, and then the lights of
everyone else who is also alone — and lines finding their way between
them.

---

**8. The monster is a word** · *SUPERBUG · UNTREATABLE · DOOMED ·
INFECTIOUS*

> "Mom, remember when we were kids and you were reading us all about
> the fact of overuse of antibiotics and that we're gonna create a
> superbug? Mom, this is what you got — a superbug!"
> — Norma's daughter

People hear "superbug" and assume no antibiotic will work at all.
Usually something still does; resistance makes the list shorter and the
treatment more complicated.

**This is the only beat with an organism in it, and it should be the
smallest thing in the frame** — the word is the monster, not the
bacterium. Do not draw a menacing microbe. The whole point is that the
fear is larger than the thing.

---

**9. The world beyond the hospital** · *Work · School · Money · Getting
there*

> "Doctors get straight to what illness, what procedure… but often this
> is a small part of a patient's day to day concern."

Eventually the patient leaves the room and the infection goes with them
— but so do work, school, dependents, relationships and money.
Appointments get pinned all through it.

*Currently:* a life laid out, with appointments punched through it.

---

**10. The whole person** · *Understanding · Support · Communication ·
Education · Connection · Care*

> "We fail to address the whole person."

Everything in this story — the ward, the family, the organism, the
courses of treatment, the walls, the distant lights — has been circling
one person the whole time.

This is the turn. The first nine beats accumulate; this one resolves.
It should feel different from what came before without changing
vocabulary.

---

**11. And it was never one person** · *(no fragment words)*

> "When we talk about our experience, we are just asking to be
> understood, to be supported." — Sunny Loo

Every light out there was somebody. Another room, another night,
another person told the same thing and left to carry it on their own.

This is the last thing a reader sees before the page asks them to do
something. It is the only beat allowed to be warm.

---

## Deliverables

Eleven files, named exactly:

```
fall  rollercoaster  weight  corridor  machine  glass
ocean  monster  world  whole  breath
```

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Format | WebP |
| Dimensions | 896 × 896 px, square |
| Colour | sRGB |
| Ground | opaque `#f4ecdb`, not transparent |
| Weight | **≤ 120 KB each**, ≤ 900 KB for all eleven |

Please also supply the layered source files.

**The weight limit is enforced by the build** and is not a guideline:
`npm run check:bundle` fails on an image over it. Flat colour and
limited palette compress extremely well in WebP — a drawing of this
kind lands near 60 KB. If something is over, it is usually a
photographic texture or a gradient that wants dithering.

They can be delivered **one at a time**. The site renders the existing
drawing for any beat whose illustration has not arrived, so there is no
all-or-nothing deadline and nothing looks broken mid-way.

### Alt text

Each illustration needs one sentence of alt text, supplied with it,
describing what is shown — not what it means. The beat's words are
already read out to a screen-reader user; the alt text should not
repeat them.

The current drawings are marked decorative and hidden from screen
readers, because they add nothing the words do not already say. A
commissioned illustration is a different object and is described. This
is checked by the build.

## What to avoid

- **No identifiable people.** The figure is featureless and stays that
  way. Two real patients consented to their words appearing on this
  site; nobody consented to being depicted.
- **No recognisable place.** No hospital, ward, or institution that
  could be identified. Real clinicians spoke to this team on the record.
- **No medical equipment drawn faithfully.** Suggest, do not render.
- **No text of any kind.**
- **Nothing frightening.** People living with these infections will
  read this page. Several told us the hardest part was being treated as
  a contamination risk rather than a person. Artwork that makes the
  reader the threat has inverted the entire argument.
- **No stock-illustration conventions** — no oversized-head flat
  characters, no abstract swooshes, no clinical blue-green.

## For whoever commissions this

**The eleven slots are already wired up.** Drop a file into
`public/stills/` and add one line to `ARTWORK` in
`src/components/home/SceneStill.tsx`; that beat switches from the
drawing to the illustration. Nothing else changes, and beats with no
image keep working.

**A note worth having before you decide who draws these.** Generated
artwork is fast and free and would fill all eleven slots this week. It
is also the wrong tool here, for a reason specific to this project: the
site's argument is that these are real people whose accounts were not
listened to carefully. A reviewer who recognises the artwork as
generated has been handed a reason to doubt that, and the review of
this site has already flagged one AI tell as a credibility problem. A
commissioned illustrator — a classmate, a student, someone local — is
slower and produces something the team can stand behind.

If budget rules that out, the existing SVG drawings are genuinely
decent and honest about what they are. Keeping them is a better
outcome than filling the slots with something that undercuts the point.
