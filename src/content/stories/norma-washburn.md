> A sinking feeling is what you feel first: I’ve had this before and oh no, it’s happening again.

## It isn’t one bad day

What gets described here is a rollercoaster, not a single bad episode: initial
hope, then despair, then encouragement, and back down again. The cycle repeats,
and it is the repetition more than any one low point that wears a person down.
It is also the part least likely to appear anywhere in a chart.

## Being told, and not being told

Appointments moved quickly. Options and side effects went unexplained. When
their daughter reached for a word for what was happening, the word was
“superbug”.

That word does damage on its own. The misconception it carries is that the
person has become resistant to antibiotics. They haven’t; the bacteria have.

## The weight of the schedule

The sheer number of hospital visits took its own toll — to the point of
collapsing under the volume of appointments. That burden sits almost entirely
outside the clinical record.

## What helped

Church, and prayer. Not something anyone prescribed or arranged; something they
found for themselves. They also told us plainly that a community for people in
their position does not currently exist.

> We fail to address the whole person.
